# 🔖 Maxima Therapy

**Take one thing.** Award-winning illustration that makes care look friendly to children, with the
text held back until the scroll animation lets you read it. It won for the jury, not for parents.

![Maxima Therapy, first screen at 1280 px](./images/maximatherapy-com.webp)

| Site | Fonts | Colours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [maximatherapy.com](https://maximatherapy.com) | Robuck Rounded, ABC Diatype Rounded 500–700 | Cream `#FFF2B7` and `#F3EFE0`, green `#00B351`, yellow `#FDCB40`, orange `#FD4401`, blue |

Maxima (Awwwards Site of the Day, April 2026) supports neurodivergent people from early
intervention to residential care. Its world is flat, primary-coloured shapes with faces: hills,
clouds, a snail, a house. For families of young children that's the right register, and it is
consistent from the first screen to the footer's giant wordmark.

What it does well is make one programme per life stage the whole navigation: 0–3, 3–18, 18–65,
65+. A parent finds their place in four choices. The illustration system is one style throughout,
drawn by a real illustrator, which is why it reads as care rather than clip art.

The page fails at its job for the person who needs it. The first screen is a moving abstract shape
with no words. Long stretches of the 9,300 px page are empty cream until the scroll animation brings
content in. Body text is 12–15 px at 1280 px. The headline face is a rounded display at 166 px. It's
a strong piece of motion design that makes a worried parent wait.

**Take:** one consistent, commissioned illustration world if illustration is used at all;
navigation by the visitor's situation. **Leave:** content that waits on scroll, wordless first
screens, small body text.
